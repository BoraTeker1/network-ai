# Customer-Discovery Interview Script (15 calls)

**Audience:** Turkish software engineers (junior–mid, based in Turkey) trying to
land a **remote or European** role.

**Length:** 15–20 min. **Format:** video or voice. **Recording:** ask permission;
otherwise take notes in the log template at the bottom.

**The one rule (the Mom Test):** ask about their **past and present behavior**, never
about the future or your idea. People lie (kindly) about what they *would* do. They
can't lie about what they *did* last week. So: "Walk me through the last time you…"
not "Would you use a tool that…". **Do not describe Network AI until the very end** —
and even then, only to get reactions, not applause.

---

## 0. Screen (before booking)

Only talk to people who fit. One DM:

> "I'm researching how Turkish engineers land remote/EU roles. Not selling anything —
> 15 min on a call about your job search? Happy to do a free hands-on outreach session
> for you as a thank-you."

Confirm they are: based in Turkey, an engineer, and **currently or recently** job
hunting for remote/EU. If not all three, thank them and move on.

---

## 1. Warm-up (2 min)

- "Tell me about your current situation — what are you working on, what are you looking for?"
- "How long have you been looking for a remote/EU role?"

*(Goal: confirm the search is real and active. If they're not actually searching, the
rest is hypothetical — note it and keep it short.)*

## 2. The story of their search (6 min) — the core

- "Walk me through **the last role you applied to**. What did you do, step by step?"
- "How did you find it?"
- "After you applied — did you do anything else? Reach out to anyone?"
  - If **yes**: "Tell me exactly what you did. Who'd you message? What did you say? What happened?"
  - If **no**: "How come?" *(Listen for: didn't know who / too time-consuming / felt
    cringe / didn't think it'd work / language nerves.)*
- "What happened with that application overall?"
- "How many applications are you sending in a typical week? How many replies?"

*(Goal: find the pain. You're listening for the gap between "I know I should network"
and "I actually do it." That gap is your product. If there is no gap — they already
network easily and it works — they are not your customer.)*

## 3. Networking specifically (4 min)

- "Last time you tried to find *who* to contact at a company — how'd that go?"
- "What's the hardest part: finding the person, knowing what to say, or actually sending it?"
- "Do you write outreach in English or Turkish? How do you feel about the English ones?"
- "Have you ever **not** sent a message you'd drafted? Why?"

*(Goal: locate which exact step hurts most. That's where your product must be sharpest.)*

## 4. Money & current solutions (3 min) — the WTP read

Ask about money **indirectly, via past behavior**:

- "Have you spent anything on your job search? Courses, LinkedIn Premium, coaching,
  resume services, English lessons, anything?"
  - If **yes**: "What did you buy, how much, and was it worth it?" *(This is the single
    most important answer. People who've already paid for job-search help will pay again.)*
- "What tools or sites do you use for the search today?" *(Kariyer, LinkedIn, Wellfound,
  Glassdoor, Discord communities…)*
- "If you could wave a wand and have one part of this done for you, which part?"

*(Goal: a real history of spending money on the problem. "I bought a $200 course" beats
any amount of "I'd pay for that.")*

## 5. ONLY NOW — react to the concept (2 min)

One sentence, then shut up and listen:

> "I'm building a tool that finds remote/EU roles you fit, tells you who to contact, and
> drafts an honest outreach message in English or Turkish — you review and send it
> yourself. What's your honest reaction?"

- Watch for **lean-in** vs. politeness. "When can I use it?" / "Can you do mine now?" =
  signal. "Cool, nice idea" = nothing.
- If they're keen: "Would you want me to run it for you this week?" → recruit for the
  **concierge** test. That commitment (their time) is worth more than a verbal yes.
- **Do not** ask "would you pay $X?" — instead: "What would make this worth paying for,
  vs. just doing it yourself?" Let them set the bar.

## 6. Close

- "Who else do you know who's hunting for remote/EU roles that I should talk to?"
  *(referral = your distribution test, live.)*
- "Can I follow up when there's something to try?" → add to the waitlist.

---

## What you're trying to learn (not what you want to hear)

| Looking for (good signal) | Red flag (be honest) |
| --- | --- |
| Active, frustrated search with a clear networking gap | "I'll probably start looking later" |
| Already spent money on the job search | Never spent a lira; treats it as free-only |
| Asks to keep using it / do theirs now | Polite "nice idea," changes subject |
| Specific painful story, named companies | Vague generalities, no concrete last-time |
| Volunteers a referral | Can't think of anyone else who'd want it |
